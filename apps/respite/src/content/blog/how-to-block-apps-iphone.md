Whether you want to limit social media during work hours, keep your kids off certain apps, or just stop reaching for Instagram every five minutes, iPhone has several ways to block or restrict apps. This guide covers everything from built-in options to third-party tools — plus a section on why you might want to consider a different approach entirely.

## Can you block social media apps on iPhone?

Yes. The quickest way is **Settings > Screen Time > App Limits > Add Limit**, then pick the **Social** category (Instagram, TikTok, X, Facebook and others) and set a daily time. When the time is up, the apps gray out. To keep yourself from tapping past it, set a Screen Time passcode as described below. The sections that follow cover Downtime, Focus modes and stricter third-party options.

## How do I block apps with Apple Screen Time?

Screen Time is Apple's built-in tool for managing app usage. It's free, requires no downloads, and covers the basics of app blocking.

### Setting Up App Limits

1. Open **Settings > Screen Time**
2. If not enabled, tap **Turn On Screen Time**
3. Tap **App Limits > Add Limit**
4. Select entire categories (Social, Entertainment, Games) or tap the category to choose specific apps
5. Set your daily time allowance (e.g., 30 minutes for Social)
6. Tap **Add**

When you reach your limit, the app's icon grays out and a "Time Limit" screen appears. You'll see options to ignore the limit for 1 minute, 15 minutes, or the rest of the day.

### Making Limits Harder to Bypass

The biggest weakness of Screen Time is how easy it is to tap "Ignore Limit." To make it harder:

1. Go to **Screen Time > Lock Screen Time Settings**
2. Set a passcode (ideally different from your device passcode)
3. **Pro tip:** Have a trusted friend set the passcode so you can't override it in a moment of weakness. They can share it with you if there's a genuine need.

With a passcode set, overriding limits requires entering the code — adding enough friction to catch yourself.

### Setting Up Downtime

Downtime blocks all apps except those you've specifically allowed during scheduled hours.

1. Go to **Screen Time > Downtime**
2. Set a schedule (e.g., 10 PM to 7 AM for sleep, or 9 AM to 5 PM for work)
3. Toggle **Block at Downtime** for stricter enforcement
4. Go to **Always Allowed** to select apps that remain accessible (Phone, Messages, Maps, etc.)

During Downtime, non-allowed apps show a time limit screen and require the passcode to access.

### Using Focus Modes

Focus modes (Settings > Focus) add another layer of control by filtering notifications and optionally hiding app pages.

1. Go to **Settings > Focus**
2. Create or customize a Focus (Work, Sleep, Personal, etc.)
3. Under **Apps**, choose which apps can send notifications
4. Under **Focus Filters**, configure which Home Screen pages are visible
5. Set automatic activation triggers (time, location, or when opening a specific app)

Focus modes are powerful because they can activate automatically and customize your entire phone experience — not just blocking apps but removing them from view entirely.

## Can Shortcuts block apps on iPhone?

Apple's Shortcuts app lets you create automations that trigger when you open specific apps. While you can't truly block an app this way, you can create friction.

### Creating an App-Opening Automation

1. Open **Shortcuts > Automation > New Automation**
2. Select **App**
3. Choose the app(s) you want to add friction to
4. Select **Is Opened**
5. Set **Run Immediately** to off (so it asks before running)
6. Add actions like: Show Alert ("Do you really need to check this?"), Wait (add a delay), or Start Timer

This approach is limited — Shortcuts automations can't reliably close an app or prevent it from opening. But they can add a moment of awareness, which is often enough.

## Which third-party app blockers work on iPhone?

For stronger blocking than Screen Time provides, several third-party apps offer more control.

### Opal

**Price:** Free basic; paid premium

Opal is the most polished third-party blocker for iPhone. Create Focus Sessions that block selected apps, with options ranging from gentle (a reminder screen) to strict (Deep Focus, which is nearly impossible to bypass).

- **Deep Focus mode** — Cannot be overridden until the session ends
- **App groups** — Block categories of apps together
- **Session scheduling** — Automatic blocking at specific times
- **Usage insights** — See your patterns and progress

### One Sec

**Price:** Free basic; paid premium

One Sec takes a different approach — instead of blocking, it shows a breathing exercise before opening selected apps. You pause, breathe, and then decide whether to proceed.

- **Breathing intervention** — Brief pause before each app open
- **Research-backed** — Based on behavioral science findings
- **No hard blocking** — You always have the choice to proceed
- **Tracking** — See how often you turn back (premium)

### Freedom

**Price:** Paid subscription (see Freedom's site for current pricing)

Freedom blocks apps and websites across all your Apple devices simultaneously. Its Locked Mode prevents you from disabling blocks until the session ends.

- **Cross-platform sync** — Block across iPhone, Mac, and other devices
- **Locked Mode** — Cannot disable blocks during a session
- **Scheduled sessions** — Automatic blocking at set times
- **Website blocking** — Covers Safari and other browsers too

### Clearspace

**Price:** Freemium

Clearspace requires you to complete a physical exercise (pushups, squats) before unlocking blocked apps. Your camera verifies the exercise.

- **Physical friction** — Exercise requirement before app access
- **Camera verification** — Ensures you actually did the exercise
- **Creative approach** — Different from other blocking methods
- **Limitations** — Impractical in public places, meetings, or bed

## Is there a gentler alternative to blocking apps?

Here's something worth considering before committing to a blocking strategy: **many people eventually stop using their app blockers.**

The psychology is straightforward. When you block access to something, your brain wants it more (psychological reactance). You start finding workarounds — disabling the blocker, using the website instead, checking on a different device. The block becomes an adversary rather than an ally.

Mindful pause apps take a different approach. Instead of preventing access, they create a brief moment of awareness before you open an app. You see a prompt — a question about your intention, a reminder of your goals, a breathing exercise — and then choose consciously whether to proceed.

The difference is subtle but significant:
- **Blocking says:** "You can't have this." → Your brain: "But I want it."
- **Mindful pause says:** "Do you actually want this right now?" → Your brain: "Hmm, not really."

Many people find a pause easier to keep up than a block, because it builds awareness rather than relying on outside restriction (see our look at [mindful interventions vs blocking](/blog/mindful-tech-interventions-vs-blocking/)). You're developing the skill of catching yourself — a skill that transfers beyond any single app.

### Respite on iPhone

Respite is a free mindful pause app for iPhone, Android and Chrome. On iPhone it is [on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/respite-screen-time-control/id6757760293), built on Apple's Screen Time API, and it adds an app lock that refreshes at midnight for daily limits. It tracks your usage with history kept on your device, needs no account (sign in only if you want sync), and helps you build awareness rather than dependence on a blocker.

Other pause-based options on iOS are One Sec (paid) and ScreenZen (free). For more, see the [Apple Screen Time alternatives](/blog/apple-screen-time-alternatives/) guide.

## What is the best way to block apps on iPhone?

The most effective approach for most people isn't pure blocking or pure mindfulness — it's a combination:

1. **Use Screen Time's Downtime** for sleep hours (hard block when you should be sleeping)
2. **Use Focus modes** during work or study (reduce distractions during focused time)
3. **Use a mindful pause app** for general daily use (build awareness throughout the day)
4. **Reserve hard blocking** for high-stakes periods (exams, deadlines, digital detox weekends)

This gives you the best of both worlds: firm boundaries when they matter most, and habit-building awareness the rest of the time.

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

**"I keep tapping 'Ignore Limit'"** — Have someone else set your Screen Time passcode. Remove yourself from the temptation loop.

**"Downtime blocks apps I need"** — Review your Always Allowed list. Add essential apps there while keeping distracting ones blocked.

**"My kids bypass Screen Time"** — Set a Family Sharing Screen Time passcode they don't know. Consider using Guided Access for younger children.

**"I just switch to Safari when apps are blocked"** — Add website limits under Screen Time > Content & Privacy Restrictions > Web Content. Or use Freedom for cross-platform website blocking.

**"I disable the blocker entirely"** — This is the core limitation of blocking. Consider switching to or supplementing with a mindful pause approach, which works with your psychology rather than against it.

## Getting Started

Start with what's free and built-in. Set up Screen Time app limits for your two or three most distracting apps. Set a passcode. Give it a week.

If you find yourself constantly bypassing limits, that's useful data — it means blocking alone isn't matching how your brain works. Try adding a mindful pause approach alongside or instead of hard blocking.

The goal isn't a phone you can't use. It's a phone you use on purpose.
