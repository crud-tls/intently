Google Digital Wellbeing ships on most Android phones, and honestly, it's fine for what it is. But "fine" only gets you so far. The feature set is limited, customization is minimal, and your usage data gets folded into Google's ecosystem. If you want more control — or you'd rather your screen time habits stay between you and your phone — it's worth looking at what else is out there.

We've put together eleven alternatives and this guide to help you pick the right one. If you're on iPhone instead, see our [Apple Screen Time alternatives](/blog/apple-screen-time-alternatives/); for Android-only picks, our [free screen time apps guide](/blog/free-screen-time-app-android/) goes deeper.

## Why look for a Google Digital Wellbeing alternative?

It's not terrible — it just doesn't go very far. Here's what we kept running into:

- Limited customization and intervention options
- No mindful interventions — only hard blocking, which tends to backfire
- Usage data gets sent to Google's servers
- Surface-level analytics without meaningful breakdowns
- Bare-bones gamification (just an unlock counter)
- Android-only, no cross-platform support
- No way to export your data or track trends over time

If even one or two of these frustrate you, there's likely a better fit below.

## 1. Respite — Best for Privacy and Lasting Habit Change


Here's the thing about most screen time apps: they block you from using your phone, you get frustrated, and eventually you uninstall them. Respite takes a fundamentally different approach. Instead of locking you out, it creates mindful interventions — gentle, full-screen reminders that help you pause and decide whether you actually want to open that app. It builds awareness rather than restriction.

What makes it stand out:

- Mindful interventions with full-screen awareness moments
- Automatic usage tracking with daily, weekly, and monthly breakdowns
- Streaks, achievements, and milestone celebrations to keep you motivated
- No account needed (sign in only if you want sync across devices)
- Available on iPhone ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/respite-screen-time-control/id6757760293)), Android and Chrome
- Free core app with no ads; an optional Plus upgrade adds sync and extras

The privacy angle is worth emphasizing. Your usage history stays on your device unless you turn on sync, and there's no account to create. For anyone who's uncomfortable with the idea of a company cataloging their app habits, that's a meaningful difference.

The one downside: if you're specifically looking for hard blocking, that's not what Respite is designed for — by intention, not limitation. (On iPhone it's built on Apple's Screen Time API and adds an app lock that refreshes at midnight.)

We think this is the best option for most people. The combination of a pause-based approach, strong privacy defaults and a free core app is hard to beat.

## 2. ActionDash — The Power User's Dashboard

If you love data, ActionDash will make you very happy. It takes what Google Digital Wellbeing does and cranks it up significantly: usage stats broken down by hour, day, and category; app launch tracking; notification counts; exportable CSVs. The Material Design interface is clean, and there's a good set of customizable widgets for your home screen.

The trade-off is that it's really just a tracking tool. If you're looking for something that'll actually help you change behavior, ActionDash won't do much — it'll just show you very detailed evidence of your habits. It can also be overwhelming if you don't enjoy digging through dashboards.

Pricing is refreshingly simple: free version for basics, $4.99 one-time for everything. It does collect anonymous usage statistics by default, though you can disable that in settings.

A great choice for data enthusiasts, but if behavior change is your goal, you'll probably want to pair it with something else.

## 3. StayFree — Solid and Free (With Ads)

StayFree doesn't try to reinvent anything. It gives you app usage tracking, daily time limits, scheduled blocking, and distraction alerts. It works, it's free, and it gets the job done for people who just want basic guardrails without paying.

The catch, predictably, is ads. They're not overwhelming, but they're there. You can remove them for a $6.99 lifetime purchase, which is reasonable. The UI isn't as polished as some competitors, and the analytics are basic compared to ActionDash, but for a free tool it covers the fundamentals well.

Worth a look if budget is your primary concern and you don't mind a few ads in your screen time app (yes, the irony isn't lost on us).

## 4. YourHour — Confronting the Numbers

YourHour does something interesting: it gives you a real-time clock timer showing how long your current session has been going, plus an overall "phone addiction" score. There's something uncomfortably effective about watching a timer tick upward while you're scrolling. It creates immediate awareness in a way that daily summaries don't.

- Real-time session timer
- Addiction scoring system
- Goal setting and motivational messages
- Notification management tools

The app feels a bit dated visually, and the free version has ads and upsells. Premium runs $19.99/year. It also collects analytics data, which is worth knowing if privacy matters to you.

If the idea of quantifying your phone dependency appeals to you, YourHour does that well. Just don't expect a modern-feeling experience.

## 5. Socratic (by Mindsettled) — The AI Coach Approach

Socratic tries to be a personal digital wellbeing coach powered by AI. You tell it your goals, it analyzes your behavior, and it gives you personalized recommendations through a conversational interface. Think daily check-ins, tailored challenges, and adaptive guidance.

It's a genuinely interesting concept. The problem is twofold: it costs $9.99/month (or $79.99/year), and all that personalized coaching requires sending your usage data to the cloud for AI processing. If you're specifically looking for alternatives because of Google's data collection, Socratic moves you in the wrong direction on that front.

For people who want hand-holding and don't mind the price or the privacy trade-off, it's worth trying. For everyone else, there are better options.

## 6. One Sec — One Simple Idea, Done Well

One Sec has the most focused pitch of any app on this list: when you open a distracting app, it makes you take a deep breath (5-10 seconds) before you can continue. That's basically it.

And honestly? It works surprisingly well for impulse control. That tiny pause is often enough to make you realize you didn't actually need to open Instagram for the fourth time this hour. It's available on both iOS and Android, the design is clean, and it does its one thing effectively.

The limitation is obvious — it really is just that one thing. No meaningful analytics, no gamification, no broader habit-building framework. At $24.99/year (or $4.99/month), the price feels steep for a single feature, especially when Respite includes pause interventions alongside a broader toolkit in its free app.

A good option if you want something minimal. Respite covers similar ground with more tracking; see [Respite vs One Sec](/blog/respite-vs-one-sec/).

## 7. Forest — Focus Sessions With a Charming Twist

Forest turns focus time into a game: start a session, and a virtual tree begins growing. Stay off your phone and it flourishes. Pick up your phone early and it dies. Over time, you build a little forest — and the coins you earn can go toward planting actual trees through Trees for the Future.

It's charming, it's motivating, and at $1.99 on Android ($3.99 on iOS), the price is right. The social planting feature (grow trees with friends) adds a nice layer of accountability.

The caveat is that Forest only covers focus sessions. It won't track your overall screen time, it won't help you understand your habits, and it won't intervene when you're mindlessly scrolling at midnight. Think of it as a pomodoro timer with personality, not a comprehensive wellbeing tool. Great for students and anyone who responds well to gamification.

## 8. Freedom — The Nuclear Option

Freedom is what you reach for when you need distraction blocking across every device you own — phone, tablet, laptop, desktop. It blocks apps and websites simultaneously across all of them, and its "Locked Mode" prevents you from circumventing it mid-session (you literally can't uninstall it during a block).

It's powerful, and for people who work across multiple devices and need enforced focus periods, nothing else quite matches it.

The downsides: it's expensive at $39.99/year ($129 lifetime, or $8.99/month), the mobile experience feels secondary to desktop, and philosophically it's pure restriction. It doesn't help you build better habits — it just makes the bad ones temporarily impossible. Once the block ends, you're back to willpower alone.

Best suited for professionals who need hard boundaries during work hours and are willing to pay for it.

## 9. Clearspace -- Physical Exercise Friction

Platform: iOS | Price: Freemium

Clearspace takes "embodied friction" literally: before you can open a distracting app, you have to complete pushups, squats, or another physical exercise verified by your phone's camera. If you want Instagram badly enough to do 10 pushups first, go ahead. Most of the time, you won't bother -- which is the point.

It's a creative concept that fitness-oriented users find genuinely engaging. The physical effort creates a level of friction that's harder to dismiss than a timer or breathing exercise. The main limitations: it's iOS-only, some features require payment, and doing exercises in public isn't always feasible.

## 10. FlowBuddy -- Free, Open-Source Breathing Pauses

Platform: Android | Price: Free (open-source)

FlowBuddy is a free, open-source One Sec alternative for Android. It triggers a breathing exercise before distracting apps open -- same core concept, no subscription, no account, no data collection. The code is fully transparent and community-maintained.

The trade-off is that it's intentionally minimal: no analytics, no gamification, no progress tracking. It does one thing cleanly and for free. If you want a simple breathing pause on Android with zero data collection, FlowBuddy delivers.

---

## 11. Refocus -- Mindful Reminders With Time Limits

Platform: iOS, Android | Price: Freemium (generous free tier)

Refocus sits between hard blocking and pure mindfulness. Set daily time limits per app, and when you approach them, Refocus sends a gentle nudge rather than cutting you off. You can keep going, but the reminder creates a conscious decision point.

Cross-platform support is a plus, and the free tier is genuinely useful. The approach blends structure (time limits) with awareness (reminders). Analytics are basic compared to Respite or ActionDash, but it's a solid middle-ground option if you want guardrails without rigidity.

## Which One Should You Pick?

It depends on what matters most to you:

**Privacy and lasting change** -- Go with Respite. It's free, needs no account, and is built around helping you actually shift your relationship with your phone rather than just restricting it.

**Detailed analytics** -- ActionDash gives you more data than you'll probably know what to do with, and the $4.99 one-time price is fair.

**Tight budget** -- StayFree covers the basics for free. Forest is $1.99 if you want something more engaging.

**Real-time awareness** -- YourHour's live session timer is uniquely effective if you respond to seeing the numbers tick upward.

**Personalized coaching** -- Socratic, if you're willing to spend $9.99/month and share your data.

**Impulse interruption** -- One Sec if you want the research-backed breathing pause and don't mind paying; Respite if you want a free pause with tracking.

**Gamified focus** -- Forest, hands down. Fun, cheap, and you get to plant real trees.

**Multi-device lockdown** -- Freedom is the only option that truly blocks across all platforms at once.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Google Digital Wellbeing good enough?

For casual users who aren't worried about privacy, it's adequate. But if you want more features, better privacy, or methods that go beyond a grayed-out icon, alternatives like Respite are worth trying.

### Do these apps drain battery?

Most modern screen time apps have a small battery impact, but it varies by app and phone. Apps that keep data on the device rather than uploading it in the background tend to be lighter. Check your phone's battery stats after a few days.

### Can I use multiple apps at once?

You can, but we wouldn't recommend it. Running multiple screen time apps creates conflicting interventions and can actually make it harder to build consistent habits. Pick one and stick with it for at least 30 days.

### Which app has the best privacy?

Respite: it needs no account and keeps your usage history on your device unless you sync. ActionDash and One Sec are also reasonable (minimal, optional analytics). Avoid Socratic and Freedom if privacy matters to you.

### Do blocking apps actually work long-term?

They're effective in the short term, but most people go back to old habits once the blocks come off. That's because blocking doesn't build internal motivation — it's just external restriction. Mindful intervention approaches (like Respite's) tend to produce more durable results because they help you develop awareness and self-regulation rather than relying on a digital lock.

### How much should I pay for a screen time app?

Respite's core app is free, so you don't have to pay anything. Beyond that, $0-10 one-time (ActionDash, Forest) is reasonable, and a yearly subscription like Freedom's is acceptable if you need cross-device blocking. Socratic at $9.99/month is hard to justify.

### Which app works best for severe addiction?

For serious phone dependency, we'd recommend combining professional support (therapy, for instance) with Respite's mindful interventions. Blocking apps might seem like the answer, but they don't address root causes and often lead to workarounds.
