If you're looking for an app blocker, you probably want to use your phone less and feel like you need something stronger than willpower. That's a reasonable instinct. But before you install a blocker, it's worth understanding the full landscape — because blocking isn't the only approach, and for many people, it isn't even the best one.

This guide covers the top app blockers for Android and iPhone, then explores a different category entirely: mindful alternatives that create a pause instead of a wall. Understanding both approaches will help you pick the right tool for how your brain actually works. If you already know you want alternatives to a specific app, see our guides to [Freedom alternatives](/blog/freedom-alternatives/) and [Opal alternatives](/blog/opal-alternatives/).

## What is the difference between blocking and pausing?

**Hard blocking** prevents access to apps entirely — either on a schedule, after a time limit, or during focused sessions. The app physically can't open. Some blockers make it deliberately difficult to undo the block.

**Mindful interventions** create a brief pause before an app opens. You see a prompt — a question, a reminder, a breathing exercise — and then decide whether to proceed. The app isn't blocked; the launch is just slowed down enough to engage your conscious mind.

Both approaches reduce screen time. They differ in whether they change the underlying habit.

## Which app blockers are the best?

### Freedom — Best Cross-Platform Blocker

**Platforms:** iOS, Android, Mac, Windows, Chrome
**Price:** Subscription, around $40 a year at the time of writing (a lifetime plan is also sold)

Freedom blocks apps and websites across all your devices simultaneously. Create "blocksessions" that sync across phone, tablet, and computer so you can't cheat by switching devices.

**Strengths:** True cross-platform sync, website blocking, scheduled sessions, Locked Mode (prevents you from disabling blocks)
**Weaknesses:** Subscription required, Locked Mode can be frustrating in emergencies, doesn't address underlying habits

### Cold Turkey — Most Hardcore Blocker

**Platforms:** Windows, Mac (phone support limited)
**Price:** Free basic; Pro is a one-time purchase of around $40

Cold Turkey takes blocking seriously. In its strictest mode, you literally cannot access blocked sites or apps until the timer expires — not even by restarting your computer or uninstalling the app. It's designed for people who know they'll try to cheat.

**Strengths:** Nearly impossible to bypass, one-time purchase, scheduling, allowlists
**Weaknesses:** Desktop-focused, aggressive approach can feel oppressive, no mobile app for direct phone blocking

### Opal — Best iOS-Focused Blocker

**Platforms:** iOS
**Price:** Free basic; Opal Pro (around $100/year)

Opal provides session-based blocking for iPhone with a polished interface. Create focus sessions that block distracting apps, with options for gentle reminders or hard blocks.

**Strengths:** Beautiful iOS-native design, group blocking sessions, usage insights, Deep Focus mode
**Weaknesses:** iOS only, premium pricing for full features, subscription model

### Apple Screen Time — Best Free iOS Blocker

**Platforms:** iOS, macOS (built-in)
**Price:** Free

Apple's built-in Screen Time lets you set app limits, schedule Downtime, and restrict specific apps. It's basic but free and requires no additional installation.

**Strengths:** Free, built-in, parental controls, Focus modes integration
**Weaknesses:** Easy to bypass (just tap "Ignore Limit"), limited customization, no mindful component

### Google Digital Wellbeing — Best Free Android Blocker

**Platforms:** Android (built-in)
**Price:** Free

Google's built-in tool offers app timers, Focus mode, and Wind Down. Similar to Apple Screen Time in scope — adequate for basic management but limited in depth.

**Strengths:** Free, pre-installed, Focus mode for temporary pausing, Wind Down for bedtime
**Weaknesses:** App timers easily overridden, minimal analytics, no intervention beyond graying out icons

## Why do app blockers stop working?

Blocking apps feels like a solution — and for a while, it works. Usage drops. You feel in control. But in practice, **many people abandon or bypass their blocker within a few weeks.** For a fuller look, see [do app blockers work?](/blog/do-app-blockers-work/).

Here's why:

### Psychological Reactance

When you're told you can't have something, you often want it more. Psychologists call this reactance. The more forcefully an app is blocked, the more your mind fixates on it. You start looking for workarounds — using a different device, accessing the website through a browser, or just disabling the blocker entirely.

### It Doesn't Address the Habit

Blocking suppresses behavior without changing it. The underlying habit — the trigger, the craving, the automatic reach for your phone — stays intact. When the block is removed (intentionally or not), the behavior comes back at full strength because nothing in your brain actually changed.

### Friction Fatigue

Blockers that are easy to bypass get bypassed. Blockers that are hard to bypass create frustration. Either way, you eventually resent the tool instead of developing a healthier relationship with your phone.

### Real Life Needs Flexibility

Sometimes you genuinely need to check the app you've blocked. A friend messages about urgent plans on Instagram. A work contact sends something through Twitter DM. When a blocker prevents legitimate access, it trains you to see it as an obstacle rather than a helpful tool.

## How do I choose between a blocker and a pause app?

### A hard blocker is right if:

- You need immediate, non-negotiable restriction (e.g., during exams, deadlines)
- You've identified specific apps you genuinely never need during certain hours
- You have a clear, time-limited use case (project sprint, study period)
- You understand you'll likely need to transition to a different approach long-term

### A mindful alternative is right if:

- You want to build lasting habits, not just suppress behavior temporarily
- You value having the choice to proceed when there's a legitimate reason
- You've tried blockers before and found yourself disabling them
- You want to understand your habits through tracking and analytics
- Privacy matters — you want your usage history to stay on your device unless you choose to sync
- You'd rather start with a free tool than pay a subscription

### Consider both if:

- You use a hard blocker for specific high-stakes windows (exam prep, deep work) and a mindful app for general daily use
- You're transitioning away from blocking and want a softer landing

## Getting Started

If you're new to screen time management, start with the mindful approach. It's less disruptive, more sustainable, and gives you data about your actual habits that pure blocking never provides. It is free and takes a few minutes to set up. The goal isn't to fight your phone forever. It's to build the awareness that makes the fight unnecessary.
