# Pawse vs FlowBuddy: Full-Featured Toolkit or Minimal Breathing Pause?

If you're looking for a free way to add a pause before opening apps on Android, you've probably come across both Pawse and FlowBuddy. Both are free, both run on Android, and both create a moment of friction before you open distracting apps.

But that's where the similarities end. Pawse is a full-featured mindful screen time toolkit. FlowBuddy is a minimal, open-source app that does exactly one thing: show a breathing animation before an app opens.

## Quick Verdict

Pawse offers customizable prompts, usage tracking, detailed analytics, gamification, and a Chrome extension — all completely free. FlowBuddy is an open-source app that provides a simple breathing pause and nothing else. Both are free and privacy-respecting, but Pawse gives you significantly more tools to actually change your habits.

**Bottom line:** If you want a comprehensive free alternative to One Sec on Android, Pawse is the better match. If you want the absolute simplest possible breathing pause with open-source transparency, FlowBuddy fills that niche.

## What Each App Offers

### Pawse: A Complete Toolkit

Pawse intercepts app launches with fully customizable interventions. You write your own messages — questions, reminders, affirmations, breathing prompts — and can set different interventions for different apps. A pointed question for Instagram, a breathing exercise for Reddit, a goal reminder for YouTube.

Beyond the pause itself, Pawse includes:

- **Usage tracking** with daily, weekly, and monthly breakdowns
- **Analytics** showing how often you proceed vs. turn back
- **Gamification** with streaks and milestones to maintain motivation
- **Home screen widgets** for quick stats
- **Chrome extension** for managing website habits on desktop
- **Works offline** — no account needed

Everything is free. No premium tier, no feature gates, no subscriptions.

### FlowBuddy: One Feature, Done Simply

FlowBuddy takes a radically different approach: do one thing and do it simply. When you open a tracked app, FlowBuddy shows a breathing animation. You breathe through it, and the app opens.

That's it. No custom messages, no usage tracking, no analytics, no gamification, no browser extension. FlowBuddy is open-source, meaning its code is publicly available for anyone to inspect. For users who value code transparency above all else, this matters.

The app is lightweight and does exactly what it promises — nothing more, nothing less.

## Where They Differ

### Feature Depth

This is the fundamental difference. Pawse gives you tools to understand and change your habits over time: tracking shows you patterns, analytics measure progress, gamification maintains motivation, and custom messages keep interventions fresh.

FlowBuddy gives you the pause and leaves the rest to you. There's no data to review, no progress to track, no messages to customize. If the breathing pause alone is enough to change your behavior, FlowBuddy's simplicity is appealing. If you need more support, you'll find yourself wanting what Pawse offers.

### Customization

Pawse lets you write personalized intervention messages, set different prompts for different apps, adjust pause durations, and choose intervention types. The experience evolves with your goals.

FlowBuddy offers the same breathing animation for every app. You can't customize what you see or tailor the experience. For some users, this consistency is a feature — there's nothing to configure. For others, a generic animation becomes easy to ignore after a few weeks.

### Open Source vs Offline Privacy

FlowBuddy's biggest differentiator is being open-source. Anyone can read the code and verify exactly what it does. This is meaningful for users who want maximum transparency.

Pawse isn't open-source, but it takes privacy seriously in a different way: it works offline, needs no account, and keeps your usage history on your phone unless you sign in to sync. It sends anonymous analytics and crash reports, and the privacy policy says exactly what.

Both approaches achieve real privacy, just through different mechanisms.

### Platform Coverage

Both work on Android. Only Pawse also offers a Chrome extension for managing distracting websites on desktop. FlowBuddy has no browser component and no iOS version. On iPhone, [Respite](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/respite-screen-time-control/id6757760293) — Pawse's iOS companion — covers the same ground.

## Who Should Choose What

### Choose Pawse if:

- You want a free app that offers more than just a pause
- Usage tracking and analytics matter for understanding your habits
- You want to write personalized messages that evolve with your goals
- You need a Chrome extension for website habits too
- You want gamification features to stay motivated
- You're looking for a comprehensive free One Sec alternative on Android

### Choose FlowBuddy if:

- You want the absolute simplest possible intervention — just a breathing pause
- Open-source code transparency is your top priority
- You don't need tracking, analytics, or customization
- You prefer a minimal app with no configuration required
- A breathing animation alone is enough to change your app-opening behavior

## The Bigger Picture

Pawse and FlowBuddy represent two ends of a spectrum. Pawse asks: "What tools do you need to build lasting habits?" FlowBuddy asks: "What's the minimum intervention that might work?"

Both questions are valid. Some people thrive with data, customization, and progress tracking. Others just need a simple speed bump before opening apps and prefer not to think about it further.

The good news is that both are free, so trying each one costs nothing but a few minutes. Start with whichever approach appeals to you, give it two weeks, and see whether the intervention is still working or whether you've started to tune it out.
