# Intently vs One Sec: Two Takes on the Mindful Pause

Of all the screen time apps on the market, **Intently** and **One Sec** are the most philosophically aligned. While other tools rely on hard blocking, timers, or gamification, these two share a core conviction: **a moment of awareness before you open an app is more powerful than a locked screen**.

That shared DNA makes this comparison unusually interesting. These aren't opposing philosophies — they're two variations on the same idea. The question isn't which one "works" (both do), but which variation fits the way your mind operates.

## The Shared Idea: Interrupt the Autopilot

Every time you reach for Instagram, TikTok, or Reddit, there's a split-second gap between the impulse and the action. Most of the time, that gap is invisible — your thumb moves faster than your conscious mind. Both Intently and One Sec exist to widen that gap, to insert a moment of awareness into the space between "I want to scroll" and "I'm scrolling."

Where they diverge is in what fills that moment.

## One Sec's Approach: The Breathing Exercise

One Sec fills the pause with a **guided breathing exercise** — a timed inhale/exhale animation that lasts several seconds. After the breathing exercise, you're asked whether you still want to open the app. The idea is straightforward: the breathing activates your parasympathetic nervous system, calming the impulsive urge that triggered the app open in the first place.

This approach has real science behind it. A **peer-reviewed study from the Max Planck Institute** found that One Sec's intervention led to a **57% reduction in social media app opens**. That's one of the strongest pieces of research for any individual screen time app — and it deserves credit. Few apps in this space can point to a published, peer-reviewed study with that kind of result.

One Sec also includes intention tracking (marking why you're opening an app) and statistics on how often you proceed versus close. The experience is consistent — the same breathing exercise every time — which makes it simple to use and requires zero configuration.

**One Sec's strengths**: Research-validated, simple setup, effective breathing-based calming mechanism, strong on iOS with deep Shortcuts integration.

**One Sec's trade-off**: The fixed breathing exercise can feel repetitive over time. Some users report that after several weeks, they begin going through the breathing motions on autopilot — the very pattern the app is designed to interrupt.

## Intently's Approach: The Customizable Prompt

Intently takes a different path. Instead of a fixed exercise, it presents a **full-screen intervention with a message you've written yourself** — a question, a goal reminder, or a reflection prompt:

- "Is this what you want to spend your time on right now?"
- "Remember: you said you'd read more this week."
- Custom messages aligned with whatever matters most to you

You always have the choice to proceed or close. There is no forced wait, no animation you have to sit through. The friction is cognitive, not temporal — it works by making you confront your own intentions rather than following a prescribed exercise.

The theory behind this approach draws on behavioral science research suggesting that **personalized interventions maintain their effectiveness longer** than generic ones, because your brain is less likely to habituate to a message that connects to your specific goals and values.

**Intently's strengths**: Fully customizable prompts, free with no limitations, 100% offline with zero data collection, strong on Android and Chrome.

**Intently's trade-off**: The open-ended nature means you need to invest a few minutes crafting prompts that genuinely resonate. Without that personalization, the experience is less targeted than One Sec's breathing exercise.

## Where They Actually Differ

Beyond the intervention style, there are practical differences worth weighing.

### Pricing

Intently is completely free — all features, no ads, no premium tier. One Sec offers a limited free version (a few tracked apps, basic stats), with full features unlocked at $4/month or a $50 lifetime purchase. One Sec's lifetime price is fair for what it offers, especially given the research behind it. But Intently's zero cost is a genuine advantage for users who want to try a mindful pause approach without financial commitment.

### Privacy

Intently operates 100% offline with zero data collection — no account, no cloud, no analytics of any kind. One Sec is also relatively privacy-conscious, but it offers optional cloud sync and requires more permissions. Both are better than most competitors on privacy. For users who treat data privacy as non-negotiable, Intently has the stronger guarantee.

### Platform

One Sec has its strongest experience on **iOS**, where it integrates deeply with Apple's Shortcuts framework. It also has an Android version and a Chrome extension. Intently is strongest on **Android** and **Chrome**, with iOS support coming soon. If you're an iPhone user today, One Sec is the more mature option. If you're on Android, Intently is the more fully-featured choice.

### Analytics and Tracking

Intently offers detailed usage tracking — daily, weekly, and monthly summaries with category breakdowns, baseline comparisons, and home screen widgets. All stored locally. One Sec tracks app opens and your "turned away" rate, with daily and weekly statistics. Intently's analytics are more comprehensive; One Sec's are more focused on the intervention itself.

### Motivation Features

Intently includes daily streak tracking, milestone celebrations, achievement badges, and streak recovery options. One Sec takes a more minimal approach — data-driven awareness without gamification elements. Whether this matters depends on whether streaks and badges motivate you.

## The Habituation Question

One of the most important considerations in any screen time tool is **habituation** — whether your brain tunes out the intervention over time.

One Sec's fixed breathing exercise is simple and effective, but it's the same experience every time. Some users report that after weeks of use, the breathing becomes automatic rather than reflective. You go through the motions without the mental pause that makes the intervention work.

Intently's customizable prompts address this differently — you can update your messages, rotate between different prompts, and keep the content aligned with your evolving goals. Whether this actually reduces habituation for a given user depends on how actively they engage with the customization.

It's worth noting that One Sec's Max Planck study measured results over a defined study period. Long-term habituation effects in real-world usage are less well-documented for either app.

## Who Should Choose What

**One Sec is a strong choice if** you're an iOS user, you enjoy breathwork as a mindfulness practice, you value peer-reviewed research validation, and you prefer a simple tool that requires no configuration. The Max Planck study is a genuine differentiator — if research credibility matters to you, One Sec has the edge.

**Intently is a strong choice if** you want full control over your intervention experience, privacy is a top priority, you're on Android or Chrome, and you want a completely free solution. The ability to write your own prompts means the app can grow and change with you.

For many users, this will come down to platform and personal preference. Neither app is categorically better — they're two thoughtful approaches to the same problem.

## The Bigger Picture

The fact that both Intently and One Sec exist — and that both work — tells us something encouraging about the mindful intervention approach to screen time. The digital wellbeing space has spent years debating whether to block, restrict, gamify, or track. These two apps suggest that the simplest idea might be the most powerful: **just pause**.

How that pause is designed matters. Whether it's a breathing exercise or a personal question, the mechanism is less important than the moment of consciousness it creates. Both apps deliver that moment. The rest is preference.
