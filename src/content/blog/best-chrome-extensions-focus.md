# Best Chrome Extensions for Focus 2026: Stay Productive Without Willpower

Desktop browsing has its own distraction problem. You sit down to work, open Chrome, and somehow end up 20 minutes into a Twitter thread about something you don't care about. Unlike phone habits (where you at least have to pick up a device), desktop distractions are one tab away at all times.

Chrome extensions can help — but they take wildly different approaches. Some block sites outright. Some add friction or pauses. Some surgically remove the addictive parts of websites while keeping the useful features. This guide covers the best options in each category.

## The Extensions

### Intently — Mindful Pause Before Distracting Sites

**Price**: Free | **Approach**: Mindful intervention

Intently's Chrome extension brings the same mindful pause approach from its Android app to your browser. When you navigate to a flagged site (social media, news, Reddit, whatever you've chosen), a full-screen intervention appears — a brief moment to take a breath and decide whether you actually want to be there.

You can always proceed. The pause isn't a block — it's an awareness checkpoint. This matters because it means you never feel restricted, which avoids the psychological reactance that makes people bypass hard blockers. The extension syncs with Intently's philosophy: build internal awareness, not external dependence.

It's lightweight, requires no account, and collects no data. The main limitation is that it's newer than some competitors and doesn't offer scheduling or time-limit features — it's purely the mindful pause.

**Rating: 4/5** — Best option for building lasting awareness. Free and privacy-first.

### StayFocusd — Time-Based Blocking (The Classic)

**Price**: Free | **Approach**: Daily time limits per site

StayFocusd has been around since 2011, and it's still one of the most popular focus extensions. The concept: you set a daily time allowance for distracting sites (say, 10 minutes combined for social media), and once you've used it up, those sites are blocked for the rest of the day.

The "Nuclear Option" is its signature feature — a one-click block of all distracting sites for a set period, with no way to disable it. For people who need hard enforcement during work hours, it's brutally effective.

The downsides: the interface is dated, the settings can be confusing, and it's easy to game (incognito mode, switching browsers). It also doesn't build any awareness — it just enforces limits. When the limit resets at midnight, old habits are right where you left them.

**Rating: 3.5/5** — Reliable enforcement for those who need it. The Nuclear Option is genuinely useful for deadline crunch.

---

### LeechBlock — Schedule-Based Blocking (Most Flexible)

**Price**: Free | **Approach**: Time-based and schedule-based blocking

LeechBlock is the power user's blocking extension. You can create complex rules: block Twitter from 9 AM to 5 PM on weekdays, allow Reddit for 15 minutes per hour, block everything during custom focus sessions. The scheduling flexibility is unmatched.

It's free, open-source, and the rule system is genuinely powerful. The interface isn't pretty, but it's functional. You can also set it to block after a daily time limit, similar to StayFocusd.

Like all blockers, the fundamental limitation is that it doesn't build self-regulation. It's external control. But if you want the most customizable free blocking extension, LeechBlock is it.

**Rating: 3.5/5** — The most flexible blocking rules available. Interface could use a refresh.

---

### News Feed Eradicator — Surgical Feed Removal

**Price**: Free | **Approach**: Removes feeds, keeps everything else

This is one of the cleverest approaches in the category. News Feed Eradicator doesn't block Facebook, Twitter, YouTube, or Reddit — it removes their infinite scroll feeds and replaces them with an inspirational quote. You can still access your profile, messages, groups, and specific pages. You just can't mindlessly scroll.

This is brilliant because it targets the specific feature that drives compulsive use (the algorithmic feed) while preserving the legitimate value (messaging, groups, specific content). You can still use social media — you just can't lose yourself in it.

The limitations: it's a cat-and-mouse game with platform redesigns (sometimes the feed returns after a site update until the extension is updated), and it doesn't cover all platforms equally well. Facebook and Twitter coverage is solid. Other platforms vary.

**Rating: 4/5** — The smartest approach for people who need social media but not the feed. Pairs perfectly with a mindful tool like Intently.

---

### Unhook YouTube — Remove the Rabbit Hole

**Price**: Free | **Approach**: Removes recommendations, autoplay, and other addictive features

YouTube is useful. YouTube's recommendation algorithm is a time vortex. Unhook YouTube lets you keep the former while disabling the latter. You can selectively remove: the recommendation sidebar, the home page feed, trending, autoplay, end screen cards, and comments.

The result is a YouTube where you search for specific videos and watch them — without the algorithm trying to pull you into an endless session. For people who use YouTube for learning, tutorials, or music but lose hours to "recommended" videos, this is transformative.

It's free, lightweight, and highly customizable. You can toggle individual features on and off to find your ideal balance.

**Rating: 4/5** — Essential for anyone who uses YouTube but struggles with the rabbit hole. Free and highly configurable.

---

### Forest Chrome — Focus Timer With Trees

**Price**: Free (limited) / ~$2 for full | **Approach**: Gamified focus timer

Forest's Chrome extension mirrors its mobile app: you plant a virtual tree when you start a focus session, and it dies if you visit a blacklisted site. Real trees get planted through a partnership with Trees for the Future.

The gamification is genuinely motivating for some people, and the environmental angle adds a sense of purpose. But it's a focus timer, not a general-purpose distraction tool — it only works during active focus sessions you choose to start. Between sessions, there's no protection.

**Rating: 3.5/5** — Fun and motivating for timed focus sessions. Not comprehensive for all-day distraction management.

---

### Freedom — Cross-Device Blocking (Premium)

**Price**: ~$40/year | **Approach**: Block sites across all devices simultaneously

Freedom's Chrome extension is part of its broader cross-device blocking platform. Block a site on Chrome and it's also blocked on your phone, tablet, and other computers. This closes the "I'll just check on my phone" loophole.

The "Locked Sessions" feature prevents you from disabling blocks once started. For people who bypass single-device blockers by switching to another device, Freedom is the most comprehensive solution.

The downsides: it's a paid subscription, it routes traffic through Freedom's servers (which some users find concerning for privacy), and — like all blockers — it doesn't build self-regulation skills.

**Rating: 3.5/5** — Best for people who need blocking across multiple devices. The subscription price is justified if you need cross-device enforcement.

---

### BlockSite — Simple URL Blocking

**Price**: Free (limited) / Premium available | **Approach**: URL blocklist

BlockSite is the simplest option: add URLs to a blocklist, and they're blocked. No time limits, no schedules, no nuance. Block it or don't.

The free version covers basic blocking with a limited number of sites. The premium version adds scheduling, password protection, and more sites. It's straightforward but lacks the sophistication of LeechBlock's rules or the awareness-building of mindful tools.

**Rating: 3/5** — Good for people who want dead-simple blocking. Nothing more, nothing less.

## Which Should You Choose?

**For building lasting habits**: Start with Intently. The mindful pause approach builds self-regulation that persists even when you stop using the extension.

**For surgical precision**: News Feed Eradicator + Unhook YouTube is a powerful combination. You keep the useful parts of social media and YouTube while removing the parts designed to trap you.

**For hard enforcement during work**: StayFocusd's Nuclear Option or LeechBlock's scheduled blocking gives you guaranteed distraction-free periods.

**For complete coverage**: Freedom blocks across all devices. Pair it with a mindful tool for the times when you're not in a blocked session.

Most people do best with a combination: one awareness tool (Intently) plus one or two targeted tools (News Feed Eradicator, Unhook YouTube) for the specific platforms that waste the most time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the best free Chrome extension for focus?

Intently is the best free option for building lasting awareness. For hard blocking, StayFocusd and LeechBlock are both free and reliable. News Feed Eradicator and Unhook YouTube are free surgical tools that target specific addictive features.

### Do Chrome blocking extensions actually reduce screen time?

Yes. Any form of friction — whether blocking, pausing, or removing feeds — reduces time spent on distracting sites. Research shows that even a brief pause before site access reduces visits by 50-60%. The key is picking an approach you'll actually stick with.

### Can I block sites in Chrome without an extension?

You can edit your hosts file or use router-level blocking, but these are technical and inflexible. For most people, a Chrome extension is the easiest and most configurable option.

### Should I block distracting sites or just remove the feeds?

If your issue is specifically the infinite scroll and algorithmic feed, surgical tools like News Feed Eradicator are smarter — they target the problem without removing the useful parts. If you have no productive reason to visit a site, blocking it entirely is simpler and more effective.
