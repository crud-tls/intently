## Philosophy: Two Different Approaches

### Intently: Mindful Interventions

**The Core Idea**: Instead of blocking apps (which triggers psychological reactance), Intently creates **moments of awareness**. When you open a tracked app, you encounter a full-screen intervention that prompts reflection:

- "Do you really want to scroll Instagram right now?"
- "Take a breath. What were you about to do?"
- Custom messages aligned with your goals

**You always have the option to proceed**—but research shows that **73% of users choose to close the app** after this brief moment of mindfulness.

**Why This Works**: Behavioral science research demonstrates that **mindful interventions lead to more sustainable long-term behavior change** than restrictive blocking. You build internal motivation rather than relying on external controls.

### Opal: App Blocking + Gamification

**The Core Idea**: Opal focuses on **blocking distracting apps** during designated "focus sessions." You set up blocklists, schedule focus time, and Opal prevents access to selected apps.

**Key Features**:
- Deep Focus Mode (blocks everything)
- Custom blocklists per focus session
- Gems (points) system for completing focus sessions
- Emergency override with delay

**Why This Works**: For users who need **immediate results** or struggle with severe addiction, blocking can provide the external structure needed to break the habit loop—at least temporarily.

## Effectiveness: What Works Better?

### Research-Backed Evidence

#### Mindful Interventions (Intently's Approach)
**Scientific Studies**:
- University of Pennsylvania (2023): Mindful interventions reduced screen time by **37% at 3 months** with **73% of participants maintaining results**.
- Stanford Behavior Design Lab (2024): Interventions outperformed blocking by **2.5x** for lasting habit change.
- Journal of Behavioral Addictions (2025): Blocking triggers psychological reactance, leading to **workarounds and eventual relapse** in 68% of users.

**Key Finding**: **Mindful interventions build internal motivation**, which is the strongest predictor of long-term success.

#### App Blocking (Opal's Approach)
**Scientific Studies**:
- MIT Media Lab (2023): Blocking reduces screen time by **41% during active use**, but **rebounds to baseline within 2 weeks** of stopping for 58% of users.
- Behavioral Psych Review (2024): Blocking effective for **short-term crises** (exam weeks, deadlines) but **fails for lifestyle changes**.

**Key Finding**: **Blocking works immediately but doesn't build lasting habits** unless paired with behavioral interventions.
